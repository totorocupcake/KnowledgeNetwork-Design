One-line: a collapsible family in the instrument palette — reach for it as soon as a flat instrument list passes ~8 rows.

```jsx
<InstrumentGroup label="lenses" open={open.has('lenses')} onToggle={() => fold('lenses')}
  count={active.filter((id) => id.startsWith('lens-')).length}>
  {lenses.map((l) => <InstrumentRow key={l.id} label={l.label} on={on(l.id)} swatch={l.color} onClick={() => toggle(l.id)} />)}
</InstrumentGroup>
```

Two layers is the limit: family → instrument. Never a third. The `count` is what makes folding safe — a collapsed family still reports how many of its members are on screen, so folding hides detail without hiding state. It sits **immediately after the label**, not flush right: once several families are open, a right-hand column of numbers is a column of orphans, and the eye has to travel a line of instrument rows to pair each number with its name. The label runs in a 76px **minimum** column, so the counts line up with each other — proximity and alignment at once. Keep family names to one or two short words and they will all fit it. When a list holds a name too long for that, widen the column for the **whole list** rather than letting one row break it — `labelWidth={FamilyColumn(FAMILIES.map((f) => f.label))}`, the same value on every group, so all the numbers move right together and stay a column. A name that still overruns pushes its own count out of line rather than truncate: one number off the line costs less than a name you cannot read. The ellipsis is the last resort, for a pane too narrow to hold the name at all.

Group names are lower case (they are not proper nouns, unlike preset names). Order families by how often they're reached for, not alphabetically. The disclosure is `caretStyle(open)` — a drawn 6px caret (two 1.5px strokes) in a 16px slot, rotated −45° closed and +45° open and children indent 16px — matching `TreeRow` exactly, so the palette and the tree read as the same kind of list. Do not pick a different step here.

`InstrumentRow` takes `disabled` for an instrument that is registered but not available on this surface — dim it rather than hiding it, so the palette still shows the real inventory.

**Open state.** An expanded family marks itself in weight, never in ink: the label goes `--fw-medium` → `--fw-semibold` and the `count` `--fw-regular` → `--fw-medium` with it — name and number are one head, they light together or not at all. The count moves one weight lower because JetBrains Mono is loaded at 400 and 500 only; asking it for 600 synthesises back to 500 and the number stays visibly unlit and the caret steps `--text-3` → `--text-2`. That puts the open head at the same weight as an ON instrument row but a step lighter in ink: findable, and never louder than the rows it contains. Do not reach for `--fw-bold` here — that weight belongs to `SectionLabel` above it. The mark is **drawn, not set** — `caretStyle` from `TreeRow.jsx`, exported so the palette and the tree cannot drift. A filled triangle carries far more ink per pixel than any letterform beside it and pulls the eye off the labels; a hollow triangle and a `›` angle quote are both too small and too oddly proportioned at this size to read as a shape. Two strokes at a right angle read light at ordinary chrome ink, and rotating one element covers both states — which also animates the disclosure. That is enough to find the open group when several are folded, and it keeps the label inside its rank — raising it to `--text-1` would make the head louder than the rows it contains.

**Ink hierarchy.** The label is a sub-head: `--text-2` at `--fw-medium` (semibold when open). It sits one step below the pane's section head — `SectionLabel`, `--text-1` at `--fw-bold` — and above the instrument rows, which are content and therefore `--text-1` again at row weight. Chrome gets quieter as it nests; content stays dark. Section heads must never be written by hand at `--text-3`: that inverts the hierarchy, making the families read as more important than the "instruments" head naming them.
